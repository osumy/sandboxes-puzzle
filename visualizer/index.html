Formal Mathematical Formulation & Parity Theorems

1. State Space & Combinatorics

The puzzle consists of an \(n \times 2\) vertical grid where each cell holds sand level \(s \in \{0.0, 0.5, 1.0\}\). The exact state space cardinality is \(|\Omega| = 3^{2n}\). For \(n=5\), \(|\Omega_5| = 3^{10} = \mathbf{59,049}\) states. All valid non-noop state transitions form a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) because sand falls strictly downward, equalizes irreversibly via side gates \((1.0, 0.0) \to (0.5, 0.5)\), or flushes permanently into the collector basin.

2. Action Physics & Discrete Cost Model

  • Top-Down Gate (Action a): Row \(r \to r-1\) in col \(c\). \(\Delta s = \min(s, 1.0 - s_{\text{low}})\). Cost: \(C_a = 1 + 4(s + s')\).
  • Collector Flush: Empties row 0 in col \(c\). \(\Delta s = s_{0, c}, s' = 0\). Cost: \(C_{\text{flush}} = 1 + 4 s_{0, c}\) (\(1.0 \to 5.0, 0.5 \to 3.0\)).
  • Side Gate (Action b): Equalizes horizontal cells \((1, 0) \to (0.5, 0.5)\) at constant cost \(C_b = \mathbf{1.0}\). All other configurations are no-ops.

3. The Cost Parity Theorem & Professor's 4-Row Benchmark

Every non-noop discrete action incurs an odd integer cost \(c_i \in \{1, 3, 5, 7\}\). Consequently, for any sequence of \(K\) actions, total path cost satisfies \(C = \sum_{i=1}^K c_i \equiv K \pmod 2\).

In the professor's palindromic initial state \([[1,0], [0,1], [0,1], [1,0]]\):

  • Baseline (without side gates): 10 actions of cost 5.0 = 50.0.
  • Side-Gate Solution (Cost 45): 11 actions (1 side gate at cost 1, 3 half-transfers at cost 3, 7 full-transfers at cost 5): \(1(1) + 3(3) + 7(5) = \mathbf{45}\).
  • Alternative Solution (Cost 46): 12 actions (2 side gates, 3 half-transfers, 7 full-transfers): \(2(1) + 3(3) + 7(5) = \mathbf{46}\).
  • Greedy Solution (Cost 47): 13 actions trapped by local heuristic evaluation into opening 3 side gates: \(3(1) + 3(3) + 7(5) = \mathbf{47}\).

4. Admissible & Consistent Heuristics

Heuristic \(h_3(s) = 5.0 \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} \sum_{c \in \{0, 1\}} (r + 1) s_{r, c}\) is strictly admissible (\(h_3(s) \le h^*(s)\)) and monotone/consistent across all 59,049 states, achieving significant search space reduction in A* while guaranteeing optimality.

5. Spring-Latch Physics Rule: Persistent Open Gates on Blocked Drops

Physical Law: If an upper cell holds sand (\(s = 1.0\) or \(0.5\)) and the lower cell is already full (\(s_{\text{low}} = 1.0\)), opening the drawer cannot transfer sand immediately (\(\Delta s = 0\)). Under the physical design of the puzzle, the mechanical drawer spring latch gets stuck, and the gate drawer REMAINS OPEN! Cost \(1 + 4(s + s) = 1 + 8s\) is incurred.
Simultaneous Cascade: When the gate beneath the lower cell (or the bottom collector flush valve) is subsequently opened, both cells discharge downward under gravity simultaneously through the open channel. Sand pools downward to fill lower cells to capacity, and the open gates snap shut once flow completes.

6. Side-Gate Strict Inactivity on Asymmetric Pairs: (0, 0.5) and (1, 0.5)

Quantization Law: Horizontal equalization (Action \(b\)) occurs exclusively between \((1.0, 0.0)\) or \((0.0, 1.0)\), producing \((0.5, 0.5)\) at cost \(C_b = 1.0\). For all other 7 configurations—specifically \((0.0, 0.5)\), \((1.0, 0.5)\), \((0.5, 0.5)\), \((1.0, 1.0)\), and \((0.0, 0.0)\)—opening the side gate produces strictly zero transfer (\(\Delta s = 0\)) and leaves cell levels untouched.
Mathematical Justification: Allowing equalization on \((0, 0.5)\) or \((1, 0.5)\) would produce fractional sand levels \(0.25\) or \(0.75\), destroying the discrete 3-level state space \(\Sigma = \{0.0, 0.5, 1.0\}\).

⏳ Sandboxes Puzzle - Physics & Rules Guide

📐 1. Grid Architecture & Sand Granularity

The puzzle consists of a vertical grid with 2 columns and n rows (default n=5, benchmark n=4). Row 0 is the bottom row adjacent to the collector basin; Row n-1 is the top row. Every cell holds a discrete sand volume s ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 1.0} (Empty, Half, Full). The goal is reached when all cells are completely emptied (0.0).

⬇️ 2. Top-Down Vertical Gates (Action a)

Opening a vertical drawer between row r and row r-1 in column c:

  • Standard Drop: Sand drops up to recipient capacity Δs = min(s, 1.0 - slow). Cost = 1 + 4(s + s'). Drawer closes.
  • Spring-Latch Stuck Open Rule: If upper cell has sand (1.0 or 0.5) and lower cell is completely full (1.0), sand cannot move immediately, but the mechanical spring latch gets stuck and keeps the drawer OPEN (cost 1 + 8s). When the gate beneath row r-1 (or the bottom collector valve) is subsequently opened, sand from both boxes cascades downward under gravity simultaneously, filling the lowest available cells first!

🌊 3. Collector Basin Flush (Row 0)

Underneath row 0, two collector valves dump sand into the infinite-capacity curved collector basin: Cost = 1 + 4s. If vertical gates above row 0 are stuck open, all sand in the connected channel cascades directly into the collector!

⇔ 4. Horizontal Side Gates (Action b)

Partition gates between Column 0 and Column 1 at each row r (fixed cost = 1.0):

  • Active Equalization: Operates strictly when one cell is 1.0 and the other is 0.0, transforming both into (0.5, 0.5).
  • Strict Inactivity (No-Op): For all other 7 configurations: (0, 0.5), (1, 0.5), (0.5, 0), (0.5, 1), (0.5, 0.5), (1, 1), and (0, 0), opening the gate produces zero sand transfer (Δs = 0) to preserve discrete ternary quantization.

🎯 5. Cost Parity Theorem

Every valid elementary action incurs an odd integer cost c ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}. Consequently, total path cost parity mirrors action count parity: C ≡ K (mod 2).
